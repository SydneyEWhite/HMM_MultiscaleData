## Multi-scale Ecological Data in Hidden Markov Models
Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) have been used in ecology to infer latent behavioral or physiological states from observed data, but standard HMMs require all data to be sampled at a common temporal resolution — a requirement that is often unrealistic in practice. Data collected at differing resolutions, referred to as multi-scale data, poses challenges for HMMs and other modeling frameworks. Several approaches exist for handling multi-scale data in HMMs: down-sampling to the coarsest resolution, summarizing fine-scale data with summary statistics, fitting separate models per variable, Hierarchical HMMs (HHMMs), and continuous-time HMMs. However, down-sampling and summary statistics may obscure behaviors at finer temporal resolutions, separate models are inferior to joint models, HHMMs require strong hierarchical assumptions that may not always hold, and continuous-time HMMs require the snapshot property to be satisfied. This thesis compares several of these approaches through the lens of movement ecology using 20 sets of data simulated based on real animal movement. The 20 simulations were then repeated with varying crossover between the parameter distributions of different states and with varying lengths of sampled data, to understand how the approaches adjust in the face of realistic ecological challenges. Results were compared against an ideal fully fine-scale model -- which solves a harder decoding problem -- and a baseline coarse-scale model using only GPS data. Within the simulated conditions studied, the simplest informative summary statistic -- the mean -- matched or exceeded other methods on average across the 20 simulations and five variations. Within the constraints of this thesis, evidence did not support the use of more complex methods, but further research is necessary to make more generalized conclusions.
